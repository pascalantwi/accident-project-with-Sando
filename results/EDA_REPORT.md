# Exploratory data analysis: Road Ranger incidents, FDOT District 2, 2022–2024

Generated by [`eda.py`](../src/eda/eda.py) from `incidents_events.csv.gz`. Figures are 300 dpi PNGs in [`figures/`](figures/), numbered as in the manuscript (Figure 1–4 main text, Figure S1–S4 supplementary) and without in-figure titles, since captions carry them (see `manuscript/research_plan.md` §6); the numbers behind them are in [`tables/eda/`](tables/eda/).

## Key findings

1. **The study area is FDOT District 2 (Northeast Florida), not the whole state.** All events fall in 15 counties around Jacksonville, Gainesville and Lake City. Duval County alone has 53% of Road Ranger incidents. The paper's title and scope in `draft.md` should say District 2.
2. **173,198 of 227,196 events (76%) are Road Ranger incident types**: disabled vehicles (101,933), crashes (44,158), debris (16,979) and abandoned vehicles (10,128).
3. **Demand is stable over time.** In-scope incidents ran at 56,767, 56,450 and 59,981 in 2022, 2023 and 2024, so a station plan built on these three years is not chasing a trend.
4. **Demand is concentrated, the precondition for station siting.** Half of all incidents fall in 145 of 983 occupied 1 km cells, and the 15 busiest cells hold 9.9%.
5. **The busiest locations form four clusters in central Jacksonville**: the Buckman Bridge on I-295 W, the I-95/I-10 downtown interchange, I-95 at Butler Blvd, and I-295 E at Butler Blvd. On I-75, the hotspot is near Gainesville (MM 375–392).
6. **Timing matters for staffing.** Weekdays average 28% more incidents per day than weekends, and the peak is 3–6 pm on weekdays. Crashes peak at 5 pm; abandoned vehicles are logged at 7–9 am.
7. **Crash events stay open far longer**: a median of 57 minutes, against 13 for disabled vehicles and debris and 5 for abandoned vehicles.

## Where the 2.7 GB file went

The raw `Incidents.csv` has 9,206,764 rows, but each row is one *update* to an incident (status change, location edit, severity, owner and so on), not an incident. A typical incident has 30 updates. Collapsing updates to one row per incident gives 227,196 incidents; the `n_updates` column across those rows adds back up to 9,206,301 cleaned rows, so nothing was lost.

Congestion, scheduled road work, visibility and bridge openings are traffic-management events, not service calls, so they are excluded from the siting analysis (`in_scope = False`).

### Trend over time
![Monthly trend](figures/FigureS2_monthly_trend.png)

No type shows a sustained rise or fall. Disabled vehicles vary most month to month (2,258–3,363 per month).

### When incidents happen
![Hour by weekday](figures/Figure3_hour_by_weekday.png)

![Hourly profile by type](figures/FigureS3_hourly_profile_by_type.png)

The busiest single hour is Friday 3–4 pm, at about 14.7 incidents per hour. Overnight, 1–5 am, runs at about 2 per hour. Abandoned vehicles spike at 7–9 am, probably because patrols find them on the first morning sweep; that reflects when they are *logged*, not when they were left.

### Where incidents happen
![Density map](figures/Figure1_spatial_distribution.png)

![Top hotspots](figures/Figure4_top_hotspot_cells.png)

The crash share separates two kinds of hotspot:
- **Interchanges** (I-95 at I-10, MLK Pkwy, Emerson St; I-10 at Stockton St) are 40–48% crashes. These need fast clearance.
- **Bridges and long rural stretches** (Buckman Bridge, I-75 near Gainesville) are 6–11% crashes and mostly disabled vehicles. Here the work is assistance and removal.

Adjacent cells such as the two Buckman Bridge cells belong to one hotspot. The network hotspot analysis (draft.md §5) will merge them properly.

![Routes](figures/FigureS4_routes_by_type.png)

I-75 is mostly disabled vehicles (76%), consistent with long-distance rural travel. I-95 has the most crashes (13,450). SR-21 / Blanding Blvd reports crashes only, which suggests that road feeds in from a different source.

### Event duration
![Duration](figures/Figure5_event_duration.png)

Duration is the time from an event's first to its last log update. That approximates how long it stayed open, not true clearance time. 3,932 events open more than 24 hours and 1,257 closed in under a minute are flagged and excluded here.

## Data quality notes for the paper

| Issue | Size | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Half of in-scope events were created by `avlrrsubsystem` (Road Ranger vehicle AVL) | 49.8% overall; 73% on I-75, 35% on I-95 | **Detection bias**: incidents are found where patrols already drive. State this in the limitations, and test whether hotspots hold when AVL-created events are excluded. |
| Severity is almost always "minor" | 99.9% | Not usable as a weight; use event type (crash vs other) instead. |
| Road surface and weather are almost always Dry / Clear | 98.8% / 98.3% | Too little variation to use as covariates; mention them descriptively only. |
| Lighting code 16 covers 92% of events; the code meanings are undocumented | 92% | Ask the data owner for the code list before using it. |
| Mile marker is missing for 53% of incidents | 53% | Use coordinates (snapped to the network) for linear referencing, not mile markers. |
| A few points lie off the interstates (local roads, stray coordinates) | small | Handled by the network snapping step (cleaning step 10). |
